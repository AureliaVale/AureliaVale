About This Book

This book began as a question a young woman asked herself while learning English — not a question anyone gave her, but one she could not put down: If I am ever asked why I did not share what I know, what will I say?

Forty years later, this small book is her answer.

Written by a retired teacher and grandmother who has lived inside the Islamic faith her whole life, Beneath the Same Sky is not a theological argument. It is not a debate. It is not written to convert anyone. It is written because every human being — wherever they were born, whatever they believe — has the right simply to know.

With the warmth of someone offering tea to a curious stranger, A Muslim Sister walks gently through the questions that matter most: What does Islam actually mean? Who is Allah? Who was Muhammad, peace be upon him — really? What do Muslims believe about Jesus? What are the five pillars, and why do they look the way they do?

The answers may surprise you. They are quieter than the world has suggested. More human. More recognisable.

What This Book Covers

  • The meaning of the word Islam — and why peace is at its heart
  • Who Allah is, and why Arabic-speaking Christians have always used that name
  • The life of Muhammad, peace be upon him — told honestly, with warmth and without myth
  • Jesus in Islam: honoured, loved, and present in the Quran more times than Muhammad himself
  • The five pillars — not as rules, but as a beautiful architecture for a human life
  • Why Islam was never sent for Arabs alone, but for all of humanity

Who This Book Is For

  • Anyone who has wondered about Islam and never had a calm, patient answer
  • Readers curious about interfaith understanding and comparative religion
  • Those who have encountered Islam through headlines and felt something was missing
  • Students, educators, and anyone seeking religious literacy
  • Non-Muslim readers who want to understand the faith of their neighbours, colleagues, or friends
"We live beneath the same sky, you and I. And this book is my invitation to you." — A Muslim Sister, from the Preface

A Note on This Book

Beneath the Same Sky is offered as a free gift — what the author calls sadaqa, an act of giving for the sake of God alone. No purchase is required. No registration. The book is available to read and download freely on the Internet Archive. It is 40 pages. It can be read in one sitting. But the questions it opens may stay with you much longer.
